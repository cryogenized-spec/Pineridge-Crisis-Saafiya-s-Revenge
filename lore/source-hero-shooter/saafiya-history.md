# Saafiya (“Fay”) — Character history and story continuity

> **Hero Shooter — internal story bible**  
> **Status:** Working canonical biography based on the director's character notes, with unconfirmed story beats labelled separately.  
> **Scope:** Character and narrative continuity only; not gameplay code, a quest specification, or a real-world biographical account.  
> **Spoiler notice:** This document openly discusses Fayeem's secrets and the Russian coercion plot.

## Character overview

Saafiya, sometimes called **Fay** in conversation, is a Cape Town woman whose composed, self-contained exterior masks a fiercely independent streak. Her character sits at the intersection of ordinary working life, an alternative/goth subculture, and the dangerous personal consequences of her relationship with Fayeem.

She is not introduced as a soldier, a professional operative, or an interchangeable armed NPC. She has a job, a family history, long-established tastes, and an emotional reason to be in Johannesburg when wider unrest begins. Her eventual involvement in the events of *Hero Shooter* grows out of that personal history.

**Name continuity:** Use **Saafiya** for the story character. **Fay** is a familiar shorthand. “Sophia” and “Sofia” have sometimes appeared in asset names and speech/transcription context, but should not silently displace the story spelling. No confirmed surname or exact age has been fixed.

## Cape Town roots and family

Saafiya was raised in a **coloured Muslim family in Cape Town**. Her mother has an **Afrikaans-speaking Cape Flats background**; her father is remembered as comparatively metropolitan, polished, and financially comfortable through his work. The different environments represented by her parents form part of Saafiya's upbringing, without reducing either side of the family to a stereotype.

She understands Afrikaans, but rarely chooses to speak it in everyday life. Under stress or in private, Afrikaans can re-emerge in her thoughts or speech; it should feel particular to her, not like a constant catchphrase.

Her religious and cultural background is part of her identity and family history, while her adult presentation is distinctive and individual. Neither her clothing nor her taste in music should be used as a shortcut for judging her family relationships or beliefs. The precise degree of her adult religious observance has **not** been settled.

## Working life and interests

As an adult, Saafiya works as a **manageress in the fuel-station/restaurant environment**. She knows how to keep a business running, deal with customers and staff, and take responsibility under pressure. She is more grounded in the practical demands of a shift than in any fantasy of adventure.

Outside work, she is drawn to **alternative and gothic fashion, heavy metal, cosplay and related scenes**. Her taste is personal rather than performative. She is also guarded about her private life: she keeps her social presence relatively closed and has a history reaching back to the **MXit era** of South African online communication.

She particularly loves her **black Ford Mustang**. Long drives, including outings along the **N2**, are part of her freedom and identity. Her driving is described as spirited and fast; this is a character trait, not an instruction for the player or an assertion that a particular drive is a confirmed scene.

## Appearance and identifiable possessions

The approved visual direction is retro-anime / early-3D-console gothic styling. Saafiya has a **medium-tan complexion**, **dark hair with burgundy-to-purple colouring**, pronounced eyeliner and dark lipstick, and a distinctive beauty mark. She wears sunglasses pushed up onto her hair, hoop earrings and other piercings, a choker with a **crescent pendant**, lace or fishnet details, a fitted gothic corset, belts and chains, and tall buckled boots. Outfits may change by scene while these identifying motifs remain recognisable.

Saafiya owns an **engraved, inherited Beretta 84FS Cheetah chambered in .380 ACP (9 mm Short / 9×17 mm)**. She carries **three spare magazines**. The pistol's sentimental significance and polished appearance are established character details; it should not be treated as a generic interchangeable prop.

**Canon decision — 23 November 2026:** The director confirmed **Beretta 84FS, .380 ACP** as final. The 84FS has a **double-stack magazine and a correspondingly wider grip** than the single-stack Cheetah variants. Older notes describing Saafiya's pistol as **7.65 mm** or single-stack are **superseded**, including any asset notes proposing the Model 82. Use **Beretta 84FS (.380 ACP)** consistently in future story scripts, dialogue, asset descriptions and visual reviews. This decision updates lore; it does not imply that every existing game-art asset has been remodelled.

## Fayeem: the relationship before the crisis

Saafiya and **Fayeem** have a complicated, **on-and-off relationship**. He is technically gifted, especially at reverse engineering and mechanical work, with a strong interest in gunsmithing and building or altering devices. Some of these interests and activities are outside formal licensed channels. His ability to solve technical problems is central to his character, but it does not make him emotionally transparent or trustworthy.

Fayeem's past includes service as a **lieutenant in the Russian military**. The exact period, unit, duties and circumstances of his departure have not been established; avoid supplying them by inference.

Saafiya has invested enough in the relationship that a trip to **Johannesburg**—ostensibly to spend time with or meet his family—feels consequential. She does not arrive in Johannesburg knowing the whole truth of his life.

## The concealed family and Russian coercion

Fayeem has concealed another relationship from Saafiya. More consequentially, he has a **young son, approximately two years old**, whom Saafiya does not know about.

A **Russian operative** knows of that hidden life and uses it as leverage over Fayeem. The blackmail pulls Fayeem back into work for Russian interests, with a stated focus on operations aimed at **Nigerian criminal activity**. The identities of the individual operative, the precise operation and its broader political or criminal structure remain open story questions.

The important dramatic engine is personal: the operative does not need to persuade Fayeem with ideology alone. He possesses information that could destroy Fayeem's relationship with Saafiya and expose a family Fayeem has kept hidden. Fayeem's choices now carry consequences for people who do not understand why he is making them.

**Knowledge separation:** The audience, Fayeem, the operative and Saafiya need not learn these facts simultaneously. Unless a scene establishes otherwise, Saafiya should not suddenly know about the other woman, the child, or the operative's terms before discovering evidence.

## The abrupt departure — developed opening sequence

A later opening treatment places Saafiya at **Fayeem's family home** when she notices he has left in extraordinary haste. Ordinary signs of departure do not add up. His **cologne and food/breakfast have been left behind**, while **clothes, a P-09 pistol, a rifle, a shotgun and his car** are missing. The contrast tells her that the departure was urgent, and probably concealed from her.

She receives an unfamiliar email on a **Pixel-like smartphone with a black glitter case and cat-ear loop**. The sender is presented as a **random alphanumeric-looking address**, not a saved contact literally named “Burner.” The intended message in the developed version reads:

> FAYEEM WAS HERE, LEFT NOW IN A HURRY. 125 COPPERLANE AVE, MEET ME INSIDE.

The message is a lead, **not proof of the sender's identity or motives**. It does not establish that Saafiya understands the Russian plot.

In the proposed visual sequence, she retrieves her engraved .380 ACP Beretta 84FS from beneath a pillow, gathers her **three spare magazines** (shown in a right-thigh garter), and leaves in her **own black Mustang**. The property is a modest, older, mine-worker-style house; storm clouds gather as she goes. One draft has her think or mutter angrily in Afrikaans, demonstrating how the language surfaces under emotional strain.

The scene's narrative progression is: **something is wrong → she notices what Fayeem took → an anonymous message gives her an address → personal hurt becomes a decision to seek him out**. Her reaction should retain suspicion, anger and emotional vulnerability, not simply turn her into a detached action-game character.

**Treatment status:** This is developed scene material from the November 2026 comic/intro iterations. Its broad action and props have been discussed in detail; final dialogue, panel layout, address usage and exact chronology should still be checked against approved cutscene art before it is presented as locked screenplay canon.

## Her place in the wider Hero Shooter story

The personal search for Fayeem intersects with the growing unrest in Johannesburg. Saafiya may encounter blockades, looters, opportunists and people trying to survive. The criminal activity around the Russian/Nigerian thread and the public disorder should not be flattened into one undifferentiated enemy faction.

Earlier story planning places her in or around a **mall confrontation and obstructed route**, and considers her as a **temporary armed companion to the main protagonist**. These are working connections, not a locked mission order. Her arc should continue to be driven by her own choices and knowledge rather than appearing only when a playable character needs assistance.

If she becomes a companion, she should remain recognisably Saafiya: capable and observant, guarded with strangers, difficult to intimidate, but not untouched by betrayal. Her personal conflict with Fayeem must remain meaningful beyond the immediate action.

## Continuity: established versus unresolved

### Established in director discussions

- Cape Town upbringing in a coloured Muslim family; Afrikaans-speaking mother with Cape Flats roots; more metropolitan, financially secure father.
- Understands Afrikaans but speaks it rarely; adult interests in alternative/goth culture, metal, cosplay and privacy.
- Fuel-station/restaurant managerial work, black Ford Mustang, fondness for driving.
- On-and-off involvement with Fayeem; his technical aptitude and Russian military lieutenant history.
- Johannesburg family-visit premise, concealed other woman and approximately two-year-old son, and Russian operative's blackmail.
- Engraved inherited **Beretta 84FS Cheetah, .380 ACP**, with three spare magazines; double-stack profile is canon.
- Distinctive gothic appearance, dark/burgundy hair, jewellery and established character-design motifs.

### Developed scenes or working connections

- Hasty departure from a family home; abandoned food/cologne and missing clothing, weapons and car.
- Anonymous alphanumeric-sender message directing Saafiya to **125 Copperlane Ave**.
- Pillow-hidden Cheetah, thigh-garter magazines, storm and Mustang departure; Afrikaans reaction.
- Saafiya's eventual mall encounter, separation or reunion with Fayeem, and temporary companionship with the protagonist.

### Not yet fixed

- Saafiya's exact age, surname, dates and full family history; exact religious practice in adulthood.
- Fayeem's Russian service dates, operative identity, the specifics of the operation and its outcome.
- Who sent the Copperlane email, why, whether it is trustworthy, and precisely where the search leads.
- When and how Saafiya learns about the other woman and child, what Fayeem admits, whether they reconcile, and her ending.
- The Beretta designation and cartridge are **resolved** (84FS, .380 ACP); older 7.65 mm/single-stack references remain only as superseded development history. Physical art alignment may still require a separate asset revision.

## Writing guidance

Write Saafiya as a **person before a plot mechanism**. Everyday competence, distance in relationships, sharp personal tastes, privacy and a mixture of attachment and anger should coexist. Her family background should appear through lived detail and speech patterns, not exposition dumps. Afrikaans should be used selectively and naturally.

In scenes involving Fayeem, preserve the asymmetry of knowledge and the discomfort of finding that someone familiar has maintained a second life. Keep the external operation a source of pressure on their choices, not an excuse to erase either character's agency.

Treat this document as the continuity baseline for future dialogue, cutscenes, concept art and character animations. Where a new script conflicts with an **unresolved** item, make the creative decision explicitly and update this file rather than letting assets or autogenerated dialogue determine the canon.

## November 2026 development addendum — read before revising older scenes

The director subsequently developed a much fuller account of the character's adolescence and a proposed KZN-first narrative structure. Preserve it in [the 24 November development notes](saafiya-development-notes-2026-11-24.md), with visual/gameplay implications in [the narrative presentation guide](../docs/story/narrative-presentation-and-sound-2026-11.md).

Key additions include her father **Faisal Rasool**, who adopted the name **Farrell**, a gradually changing religious and intellectual outlook, and a practical operations career; a possible later murder linked to reporting misconduct; her Afrikaans-speaking mother with potential police/K9 history; a businesswoman aunt with a Victorian/Edwardian Cape Town guesthouse and prepared cottage; an English-medium independent-school period in Grades 10–12; the **Ambrosia** reading/makeup circle and a gradual interest in modern nature-centred pagan ideas; the **single left-side black dragon-wing tattoo** with vertical spaced lowercase **vitalus** lettering; and a discreet older acquaintance she is introduced to **only after turning 18**.

**Important: naming is deliberately unresolved.** The newer proposal explores *Sofia* as a birth name and *Sophia* as a school form, while this document and current code still use **Saafiya/Fay**. Keep the existing designation pending an explicit director decision; do not infer that the words are interchangeable etymologically.

**Important: geography is deliberately unresolved.** The director now favours **KZN as the origin**, later broadening to Gauteng and organised-crime plots. Earlier chapters and the Johannesburg/Fayeem family visit still exist. Do not overwrite their dates or settings before making a unified timeline.

**Important: fictional violence and power dynamics.** The mall confrontation, Domino (Ernest Meintjies), interaction with Russian, institutional consequences and the devastating **“But None of Us Will Win”** path are story-development ideas, not a final mission script. Avoid treating ethnicity or political allegiance as a proxy for culpability. An older acquaintance's private adult master/submission relationships are separate from pagan spirituality; Saafiya's consent or participation is not established.
