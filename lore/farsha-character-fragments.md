# Farsha — recovered character development fragments

**Recovered 28 November 2026 from earlier late-November story discussions.** This character's development was not adequately represented by the first cross-project consolidation. The notes are intentionally incomplete, may contain conflicting proposals, and must not be treated as a locked screenplay.

**Status:** **[D]** director-described character idea; **[P]** tentative narrative/detail; **[O]** unresolved; **[I]** assistant interpretation awaiting adoption.

## Childhood and family

- **[D/P]** Farsha came from an **Afrikaans-speaking household**. The early years may have been genuinely happy: affectionate parents, toys, dolls and an imaginative childhood. A possible **lost twin** is a formative but *not-yet-final* idea; avoid treating the manner or timing of the loss as decided.
- **[D/P]** As family relationships gradually change, her parents may become less emotionally available. She increasingly turns toward solitary play, notably elaborate fantasies enacted with **Barbie dolls**. Do not flatten her parents into villains or treat imaginative play as evidence of pathology.
- **[D]** At an English-medium school, a pronounced **Transvaal Afrikaans accent** becomes a target of ridicule. She responds by withdrawing into silence and trying to disappear into the background. Her fluency and ease with written English eventually become different from her spoken social confidence.
- **[O]** Birthplace, current city, ages at each incident, family names, twin's fate, school transfers and exact chronology are not set.

## Discovery of MXit / Dark Mountain

- **[D/P]** She first encounters mobile internet/chat indirectly through **boys playing chess at a library** and a **Nokia/GPRS** connection. Later she has access to a phone of her own, allowing a freer private online identity.
- **[D]** Around **age sixteen**, she enters Dark Mountain. At first she mainly observes the roleplayers and their social codes before contributing.
- **[D/P]** She adopts lowercase **farsha**, associated in the recollection with **Fasha**, a *Dragon Ball* character in Bardock's group. Confirm how directly this fictional-name inspiration carries into the character; preserve the spelling as a working handle.
- **[D/I]** Online writing provides a setting in which accent, social inhibition and familiar school hierarchies no longer determine her first impression. This does not automatically make her benevolent or well-adjusted.
- **[D]** The director distinguishes the groups' origins: **Ambrosia was an offline friend circle that later migrated online; Farsha's clan originated within Dark Mountain**, and only later developed offline bonds or identity.
- **[O]** Name of Farsha's clan, its members, status, rivalry with Ambrosia, and the extent of any offline meeting remain unassigned.

## Farsha and Saafiya at school

- **[D/P]** Saafiya initially encounters a group of girls who may **appear friendly while concealing hostile or manipulative motives**. Farsha's role among them needs precise staging and should not erase Saafiya's separate genuinely kind early school friends.
- **[D]** Farsha becomes fascinated by Saafiya's confidence, possessions, personality and social identity, in ways that can turn intrusive or disturbing. Earlier descriptions explored contradictory impulses: rivalry, envy, identification, longing for closeness and a wish to adopt pieces of Saafiya's life.
- **[P]** The extent and nature of Farsha's attachment—obsessive, hostile, dependent, romantic, or some combination—remain narrative questions. Her history explains possible vulnerabilities but does not excuse coercion or abuse.
- **[D/P]** A proposed **school lavatory confrontation** occurs after Saafiya detects or suspects surveillance. The mirror is part of the scene's reveal or recognition. The encounter escalates suddenly: Saafiya strikes Farsha once; Farsha falls and sustains a serious head injury with bleeding. Preserve the distress and consequences; do not turn this into a triumphant combat tutorial.
- **[D/P]** Farsha does not necessarily retaliate. One version has her returning quietly and developing a more complicated attachment instead. Her motivations, medical outcome, what staff discover, and any repercussions for Saafiya are **[O]**.
- **[I]** This confrontation can later challenge Saafiya's early belief that winning an encounter settles the underlying problem. It would provide a concrete test of her eventual fairness and restraint, but should not be imposed as the only interpretation of the relationship.

## Relationships, thematic role and restraint

- Farsha is **not** simply an evil foil or disposable antagonist. The director's earlier concepts make her a person formed by childhood pleasures, emotional isolation, classroom shame, online freedom and badly directed attachment.
- Her loneliness should not be equated with violence or mental illness. Give her independent motivations, accomplishments and relationships.
- **[O]** Does she intersect with the historical-feeling Dark Mountain clan conflicts, the fictional Ambrosia community, or both? Is she a rival to Saafiya before Sarah's introduction, afterward, or across several stages? No final chronology has been selected.
- **[O]** Does her history lead to reconciliation, parallel lives, further escalating tensions or deliberate separation? Preserve the variants until the director chooses.
- Keep the director's real recollections of Dark Mountain and the fictional Farsha storyline **separate**; matching names, social patterns or clans are not automatic biographical identifications.

## Third-pass recovered specifics (28 November 2026)

- **[D]** Farsha's school silence is an **avoidance response to bullying over her Transvaal Afrikaans accent**, rather than contemplative mindfulness or a sign of gifted wisdom. She actively tries to fade into the background.
- **[D/P]** The library discovery involved **boys playing chess on a roll-up board**, using chess pieces stored in an **ice-cream tub**. This concrete ordinary setting can precede her learning about GPRS/MXit.
- **[D/P]** A possible device progression is **borrowed mother's mobile phone -> later Nokia from father**, enabling more private access. No exact handset model/time fixed.
- **[D/P]** Her written-English confidence develops with online participation; some late-night Dark Mountain scenes involve ritual-styled **roleplay incantations**, not necessarily literal magic in the story.
- **[O]** Keep the chess, first handset and initial online-clan meeting as fragments until the full age/school chronology is chosen. Details and alternate scene-treatment links: [third-pass fragments](third-pass-fragments-2026-11-28.md).
